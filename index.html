WHY PARROTS MATTER

A colorful world worth protecting.

Parrots are among the most recognizable and diverse groups of birds in the world. Found across tropical and subtropical regions, they display remarkable variation in size, diet, habitat, and physical characteristics.

They are responsible for transporting and dispersing seeds, their messy eating gives ample food for ground dweller species, they eat plant parasites and insect pests, they serve as pollinators, and they are an indicator species (meaning that a decline in the parrot population is a sign of environmental stress, deforestation, or climate change).

However, many parrot species face growing conservation challenges. Habitat loss, environmental displacement, and other threats have placed some species at considerable risk of extinction.

By examining the characteristics of different species, we hope to better understand potential patterns associated with extinction vulnerability.

ABOUT OUR PROJECT

Exploring biodiversity through data.

This project uses data visualization to explore patterns in parrot biodiversity and conservation. Through interactive graphics and data-driven storytelling, we aim to make complex environmental data and relationships easier to understand.

01. What have we done so far?

We have done a large amount of work thus far! The first step was procuring the dataset and doing research on the bird species and traits. Next, we set up this customized website, choosing a rainforest/parrot theme/palette. Lastly, we created some dynamic visualizations which aim to analyze characteristics of parrots that may be associated with extinction.

02. What will be the most challenging part?

There are some design challenges that we anticipate in the future. For starters, analysis may be made difficult by species that are Data Deficient or Not Evaluated by IUCN standards. While our dataset is large, there are also several missing or null values that could impact our data. Lastly, we are currently unsure if analyzing parrot species that are already extinct would be fair or biased to our analysis.
